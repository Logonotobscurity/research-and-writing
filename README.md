# Research & Writing

A durable notebook for research, writing, conceptual development, and the evolution of ideas across conversations.

## Editorial principles

- Preserve the development of an idea, not only its final formulation.
- Distinguish **user statements**, **assistant interpretations**, **joint conclusions**, and **open hypotheses**.
- Never present a reconstructed summary as a verbatim transcript.
- Keep uncertainty visible. Separate verified facts, reported claims, inference, hypothesis, assumption, and unknown.
- Record disagreements and revisions rather than silently overwriting earlier positions.
- Use evidence → hypotheses → tests → conclusions when developing research.
- Treat each distinct conversation as a self-contained folder, while allowing account-wide synthesis documents to connect recurring themes.

## Archive status

These materials are structured research records, **not complete raw exports of every message**. They are based on the conversation context and project details available at the time of writing. Synthesised passages are labelled as such; exact quotations are used only where exact wording is available. Project states and historical metrics must be reverified before external use.

Future additions should preserve exact source text when available, append new dated phases, and record what changed and why rather than silently rewriting the historical record.
