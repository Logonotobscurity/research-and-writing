### 3.3 Assistant research response — 6 November 2026

The repository does not contain the assistant's full original research response or its complete source-by-source discussion. The following is the exact research summary currently preserved in Phase 02, reproduced here as an archival extract.

#### Cognitive dissonance (Festinger 1957)

> Discomfort from conflicting beliefs or between belief and behaviour. Marketing applications create a mild gap between current state/identity and a desired one, then offer resolution. Classic examples: Apple “Think Different”, Dove Real Beauty, L’Oréal “Because you’re worth it”.

#### Identity labeling / self-perception (Bem 1967)

> People infer attitudes from their own behaviour and act consistently with labels applied to them. Noun labels (“voter”) outperform verb descriptions. Soft invitations (“you’re the type who…”, “if you’re someone who…”) create voluntary self-categorisation.

#### Commitment and consistency (Cialdini)

> Small voluntary agreements increase the likelihood of subsequent consistent action (foot-in-the-door: Freedman & Fraser 1966). Micro-commitments (continuing to read, answering a reflective question) function as identity-consistent steps.

#### Social-norm contrast

> Contrasting the reader with “most people” creates an attractive in-group identity without fabricating social proof.

#### Evidence status and constraints preserved in Phase 02

- Mechanisms and classic campaigns: **Assistant research** (multiple web sources retrieved during the conversation; see the source registry).
- Practical application to attention lines: **Joint working product** of the conversation.
- Specific campaign performance claims: treated as **Reported** unless independently verified in the thread.
- Prioritise originality and subtlety.
- Avoid illegal, harmful, or overly manipulative content.
- Keep identity voluntary.
- Ground in real patterns; verify claims.
