**Investigate and understand → define or redefine the problem → design architecture → build and integrate → validate → identify further opportunities.**

## 4. What this phase changes

The archive adds a durable layer to the user's research and development process.

Previously, conversation knowledge was distributed across chats, project notes, repositories, and memory summaries. The new structure creates a navigable record of how the ideas and systems relate.

The most important design decision is to preserve both:
- **Chronology:** how interpretations and priorities evolved.
- **Thematic depth:** what each research or product area contains.

This avoids two failure modes:
- A chronological transcript that is hard to use as a reference.
- A thematic summary that hides how and why the user's thinking changed.

## 5. Immediate forward research agenda

The following agenda is a synthesis of the gaps and priorities recorded across the workstreams. It is not a claim that all items have been approved as the user's next actions.

### Priority 1 — Make the archive a reliable source of context

- Add raw conversation exports where available.
- Add source IDs and exact dates to individual claims.
- Distinguish what was directly said from assistant synthesis.
- Record corrections and changed decisions as dated events.
- Link every project dossier to its current source-of-truth repository and specifications.
- Create a missing-source register.

### Priority 2 — Select one practical commercial proof

- Choose one business workflow with a clear customer.
- Establish a baseline and expected result.
- Deploy the smallest real workflow.
- Measure operational and commercial impact.
- Record delivery cost and user feedback.
- Produce a case study only with appropriate evidence and permission.

### Priority 3 — Validate the execution kernel

- Inspect current implementation.
- Test service-level permissions and tenant isolation.
- Test stale and forged approvals.
- Test replay, concurrency, and partial side effects.
- Verify external results and ambiguous timeouts.
- Ensure every consequential action has traceable evidence.

### Priority 4 — Turn PAL and voice into reproducible research

- Validate annotations with native speakers.
- Use actual published dataset schemas and splits.
- Remove or clearly isolate mock evaluation paths.
- Publish benchmark data and limitations.
- Separate speech recognition, pragmatic meaning, policy, and action outcomes.
- Measure unsafe action rate and clarification quality.

### Priority 5 — Reassess product portfolio readiness

For each product, inspect the repository and identify:
- Real end-to-end user journeys.
- Missing or mocked functionality.
- Hosted CI status.
- Security and privacy gaps.
- Adoption evidence.
- The smallest next useful experiment.

### Priority 6 — Turn research intelligence into decisions

- Choose the target opportunity categories.
- Establish primary-source coverage.
- Verify eligibility and deadlines.
- Measure signal precision.
- Track which opportunities produce action and outcome.
- Reduce noise and duplicate discovery.
