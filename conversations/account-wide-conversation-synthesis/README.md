## Why this folder exists

The existing conversation folder, [Research-Led Systems Builder](../research-led-systems-builder/README.md), records one specific discussion in depth. This folder has a different scope: it brings together the wider body of work visible across the account context and organizes it so that a reader can understand both the individual workstreams and the relationships between them.

It is deliberately not one long chronological transcript. The archive has two complementary navigation layers:

1. **Chronological:** how the conversation and working model developed across phases.
2. **Thematic:** what was explored, specified, challenged, built, or left unresolved in each domain.

The documents should be read together. A product specification may rely on the research method; a voice-action system may rely on PAL, execution-kernel policy, and consent; LOG_ON's commercial strategy is related to, but not identical with, the technical platform architecture.

## The archive's core thesis

Across otherwise different conversations, a recurring design question appears:

> How can intelligence become useful in the real world without becoming unaccountable?

The user repeatedly explores the connection between understanding, context, authority, action, and evidence. The projects vary, but the recurring requirements are consistent:

- Understand the problem before deciding what to build.
- Preserve context and provenance rather than flattening meaning.
- Separate model capability from permission to act.
- Keep deterministic controls outside probabilistic generation.
- Treat execution as consequential and verifiable.
- Make uncertainty visible.
- Turn research into testable mechanisms and useful systems.
- Measure actual adoption and outcomes rather than treating plans or architecture as proof.

## Interpretation and provenance rules

This archive is a reconstructed synthesis from the conversation context, saved memory, recent-conversation summaries, project references, and the repository material inspected while preparing it. It is **not a raw export of all account conversations** and does not claim complete message-by-message coverage.

Every substantive statement should be understood using the following distinctions:

- **User-reported / user-defined:** a preference, goal, project description, requirement, or result attributed to the user in the available context. This does not automatically mean independently verified.
- **Repository-observed:** a fact directly observed in a repository file, branch, commit, or other inspected artifact.
- **Assistant synthesis:** an analytical organization of multiple discussions; useful, but not a verbatim user statement.
- **Joint working conclusion:** a formulation developed through discussion and later clarified or accepted by the user.
- **Inference:** a reasoned explanation that goes beyond what was directly stated.
- **Hypothesis:** a proposition that requires an experiment or external evidence.
- **Unknown / not inspected:** information that is unavailable or has not been checked.
- **Unverified implementation claim:** a feature, result, metric, or status reported in discussion but not independently tested in this archive pass.

No reconstructed wording should be presented as a verbatim quotation unless the exact wording is available in the source context. The existence of a repository, branch, draft, specification, or code path must not be used as proof that a product is production-ready or that its claimed outcomes have been measured.

## How this archive is arranged

- The top-level documents explain the shared intellectual approach and the cross-cutting research, commercial, and technical systems.
- The products subfolder preserves product-specific purpose, scope, architecture, status, dependencies, and open questions.
- The chronology document records the broad evolution of the conversation. Where exact dates are available, they are used. Where chronology is approximate, that is stated.
- The latest-phase document records the most recent development in the available history, including the 8 November 2026 clarification about investigation and systems building and the present decision to create this archive.

## Maintenance protocol

1. Add new dated notes or phase documents; do not rewrite historical decisions as though they were always known.
2. Record the trigger for each important change: new evidence, user correction, implementation discovery, failed test, changed priority, or new opportunity.
3. Keep intended design separate from implemented behaviour and measured outcomes.
4. Link related documents rather than duplicating large sections.
5. Keep contradicted, superseded, and unresolved ideas visible.
6. When new source material becomes available, update the relevant claim and provenance notes.
7. Preserve exact user wording only when the original text is available.
8. Do not turn a hypothesis into a fact merely because it appears repeatedly in the archive.
