# 06 — Open Questions and Next Research

## Purpose

This document prevents the current working model from becoming a closed or self-confirming personality story. The model should remain open to evidence, examples, and counterexamples.

## Questions to investigate

### 1. How does the user decide when investigation is sufficient?

- What evidence makes a problem sufficiently understood to begin architecture?
- Which unknowns must be resolved before building?
- Which unknowns can be explored through a prototype?

### 2. How does the user balance discovery and delivery?

- What signals indicate that a new opportunity deserves a separate investigation?
- How are scope changes distinguished from useful discoveries?
- What practices help preserve delivery while remaining open to new findings?

These are open questions, not assumptions that the user struggles with execution.

### 3. What makes an integration especially interesting?

- Is the attraction primarily technical interoperability, emergent capabilities, workflow coordination, or the architecture of the whole?
- How does the user recognise when a collection of connected tools has become a coherent system?

### 4. How are research contributions evaluated?

- What makes an insight genuinely novel or useful?
- What evidence would validate the proposed mechanism?
- How should research claims translate into testable system behaviour?

### 5. How does the working pattern vary by context?

The balance between research, architecture, building, and integration may differ across:
- New product exploration.
- Existing-system diagnosis.
- Research and writing.
- Agent and automation architecture.
- Multilingual action systems.
- Business operating systems.
- Security, assurance, and governance.

## Suggested research method

For future examples, record:
- Context and initial problem.
- What was known and unknown.
- Investigation performed.
- Complexity or mechanisms discovered.
- Changes to the problem definition.
- Architecture and build decisions.
- Integrations made.
- Validation evidence.
- New opportunities discovered.
- What would disconfirm the current working model.

## Research discipline

Use the user's preferred evidence distinctions:
- **Verified:** directly supported by reliable evidence.
- **Reported:** stated by a participant or source but not independently verified.
- **Inference:** a reasoned interpretation from available evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** a proposition that needs testing.
- **Assumption:** a premise temporarily accepted.
- **Unknown:** information not yet available.
