# 02 — Identity-Tension Writing Framework

## 1. Development

The user developed and refined a reusable approach called **Identity-Tension Writing**. It began from interest in identity-led communication, value-action discrepancy, cognitive dissonance, and persuasive website content. It was later broadened into a context-adaptive doctrine for business, research, leadership, product UX, editorial writing, and interpersonal communication.

The mature principle is:

> **Make the important truth difficult to overlook without making the reader feel cornered.**

The shift matters: the objective is not to weaponise cognitive dissonance or pressure people into compliance. It is to make a real and relevant discrepancy visible while protecting dignity and choice.

## 2. Core sequence

**Value or intention → observable reality → meaningful discrepancy → honest interpretation → credible path → voluntary choice.**

1. Recognise what the reader, person, or organisation is trying to protect or achieve.
2. Describe what happened or what the evidence shows.
3. Identify the gap between the stated value, promise, standard, or expectation and observable behaviour or outcome.
4. Explain the implication fairly, without claiming more than the evidence supports.
5. Offer a practical path to respond, learn, repair, decide, or improve.
6. Preserve the reader's freedom to disagree, decline, or choose another route.

## 3. Tension mechanisms

Possible discrepancies include:
- Value and behaviour.
- Intention and outcome.
- Standard and system.
- Belief and evidence.
- Promise and delivery.
- Priority and trade-off.
- Role and responsibility.
- Boundary and behaviour.
- Claim and counterexample.

Identity is context, not a weapon. Do not assign an identity to a reader to compel a decision.

## 4. Evidence categories

Use Verified, Reported, Inference, Hypothesis, Assumption, Unknown, and Disputed. Do not manufacture a discrepancy, turn an inference into a fact, or use silence, uncertainty, vulnerability, or belonging as leverage.

## 5. Intensity levels

| Level | Use | Constraint |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — Neutral | Informational, sensitive, or high-stakes | Prioritise clarity and minimise pressure. |
| 1 — Gentle recognition | Public first contact and ordinary marketing | Surface a fair, understandable tension. |
| 2 — Direct reflection | Context supports a more direct challenge | Show evidence and offer a credible path. |
| 3 — Strong accountability | Justified critique or serious promise-delivery gap | Challenge behaviour or systems, never human worth. |

Choose intensity based on context, relationship, stakes, evidence quality, and likely impact.

## 6. Draft composition

A useful pattern is recognition → reality → discrepancy → consequence → fair interpretation → resolution → invitation. Not every context requires all seven moves. Technical documentation, research reports, and short personal messages may need different forms while preserving the same principles.

## 7. Quality-control scorecard

Score each dimension 0–2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Truth | Unsupported | Partly grounded | Evidence-aligned |
| Recognition | Misreads context | General | Specific and fair |
| Tension | Manufactured or absent | Imprecise | Meaningful and proportionate |
| Resolution | No credible path | Vague | Practical |
| Agency | Coercive | Some pressure | Voluntary choice |
| Craft | Confusing | Understandable | Precise and memorable |

Aim for 10–12/12. A zero in Truth or Agency is a hard stop.

## 8. Empirical caution

Identity-labeling, commitment, consistency, cognitive dissonance, and psychological reactance have been referenced in the framework's development. Effects vary by context; freedom-threatening language can create resistance. Do not repeat numerical effect estimates or meta-analysis conclusions from a notebook without checking the primary source.

## 9. Applications and doctrine

Applications include marketing, website copy, onboarding, leadership, accountability, research communication, proposals, interpersonal writing, and consent-oriented UX. For high-stakes topics, default to Level 0 or 1.

The mature framework combines truth, dignity, proportionate tension, a credible path, and reader agency. Future refinements should preserve this ethical boundary.
