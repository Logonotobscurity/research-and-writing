# ATS and Career Intelligence — Product Dossier

## 1. Product intent

The user's career-related work includes resume positioning, ATS alignment, job-market analysis, and a broader career-intelligence idea.

The intended system should not simply rewrite a CV to mirror job descriptions. It should help a candidate understand the role, identify genuine evidence of capability, detect gaps, and make better application decisions.

## 2. User problem

Candidates often struggle to:
- Understand the requirements behind job advertisements.
- Translate actual experience into relevant evidence.
- Distinguish essential criteria from desirable ones.
- Present technical work clearly.
- Identify missing proof.
- Prioritize opportunities.
- Adapt an application without exaggerating.
- Learn which changes improve interview or hiring outcomes.

## 3. Product principles

- Never invent employment, skills, qualifications, or outcomes.
- Separate evidence from inferred suitability.
- Preserve the candidate's actual experience.
- Highlight missing information rather than fabricating it.
- Explain why a change is recommended.
- Treat ATS compatibility as one part of application quality, not the whole hiring process.
- Avoid claims that an automated score predicts hiring success unless validated against real outcomes.

## 4. Proposed workflow

1. Ingest the candidate's CV and supporting evidence.
2. Parse the target job description.
3. Extract role requirements and seniority signals.
4. Map candidate evidence to each requirement.
5. Classify matches as direct, transferable, weak, missing, or unknown.
6. Identify gaps and evidence that could strengthen the application.
7. Produce a tailored but truthful CV or application.
8. Validate factual accuracy and formatting.
9. Track application outcome and feedback.
10. Use observed outcomes to improve future recommendations.

## 5. Candidate evidence model

A useful evidence record includes:
- Claim or competency.
- Source document or project.
- Context.
- Candidate's role.
- Action performed.
- Tool or method used.
- Result.
- Measurement method.
- Verification status.
- Date and freshness.

A resume metric remains reported until the candidate can explain how it was measured and provide appropriate supporting evidence.

## 6. Experiment design

Earlier research notes proposed a randomized or matched application test:
- Compare two truthful application variants for similar opportunities.
- Keep role fit and application context as comparable as possible.
- Record the changes made.
- Track callback or interview outcomes.
- Avoid attributing every difference to wording when roles, timing, referrals, and hiring processes differ.

This is an experiment proposal, not a proven method. It requires enough observations and careful control of confounding factors.

## 8. Open questions

1. Which roles best match the user's demonstrated experience?
2. Which achievements have verifiable evidence?
3. Which skills are demonstrated in repositories or deployments rather than only listed?
4. How can the system compare roles by fit and expected effort?
5. What is the right measure of application quality?
6. How should market demand and geographic constraints affect prioritization?
7. Can the experiment obtain enough comparable applications to be informative?

## 9. Current working conclusion

Career intelligence should improve evidence quality and decision-making, not manufacture a more attractive candidate. The strongest output is a truthful mapping between role requirements and demonstrated experience, supported by concrete project evidence.
