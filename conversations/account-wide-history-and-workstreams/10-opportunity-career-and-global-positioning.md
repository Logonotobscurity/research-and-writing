# 10 — Opportunity Intelligence, Career, and Global Positioning

## 1. Opportunity-intelligence purpose

The user has requested an opportunity-scanning process for programmes, grants, fellowships, jobs, open-source opportunities, and market openings—especially those connected to AI agents, safety, cybersecurity, automation, open source, research, and SME transformation.

The preference is not a long list of generic opportunities. The user wants evidence-based prioritisation and a clear path from discovery to action.

## 3. Evaluation model

A 0–100 fit score and a 90+ high-priority threshold were discussed. An assessment should consider:
- Eligibility and location.
- Deadline and current status.
- Fit with the user's actual work.
- Required skills and evidence.
- Financial and strategic value.
- Time and application effort.
- Organiser credibility.
- Likelihood of completion or selection.
- Network, market, or research leverage.
- Whether a concrete next step is possible.

Explain the score; do not hide a disqualifying eligibility issue behind a high number.

## 4. Freshness and verification

A previous preference was for opportunities posted within the last 24–48 hours where possible. This is a freshness preference, not a reason to discard strong opportunities with longer windows.

Before calling an opportunity open:
1. Check the official source.
2. Confirm publication/update date.
3. Confirm deadline and time zone.
4. Confirm eligibility.
5. Check the application portal.
6. Identify required documents and effort.
7. Separate official information from third-party commentary.

Historical opportunity names included AWS Agentic AI grant, Chapel Hill Denham MDP 2027, YALI East Africa, Youth in Policy Fellowship, and a Climate Risk Lab data science/machine learning fellowship. This archive does not state that any remains open.

## 7. Career strategy

A credible narrative should connect problem discovery, systems investigation, architecture, full-stack delivery, agentic workflows, integration, security, governance, product outcomes, and research. Avoid positioning the user as only a prompt engineer or generic automation freelancer where evidence supports deeper systems work. Equally, do not overstate seniority or capability beyond verifiable delivery.

## 8. Opportunity-to-action workflow

Each scan should end with:
- Confirmed eligible and open opportunities.
- Best-fit priority and rationale.
- Required evidence and documents.
- Application or outreach action.
- Deadline and owner.
- Opportunities to defer or reject, with reasons.

A discovery list that never becomes a decision does not complete the user's requested workflow.
