### [ITEM 1] — Arceris launches a self-hosted AI security control plane for agents and MCP

- **Source:** Arceris official website — https://arceris.ai/ ; launch-date listing: https://avalwhub.com/p/arceris
- **Author:** No named author on the reviewed product page.
- **Published:** Official product page has no visible publication timestamp. Avalwhub records the product as launched **22 November 2026**. Exact launch time and exact rolling-window qualification: **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Event date:** 22 November 2026 according to the launch directory; independently unconfirmed.
- **What happened:** Arceris describes a self-hosted AI security control plane that discovers AI applications, agents, skills, plugins and MCP servers across an organisation. Its stated controls include policy enforcement across browser, terminal and IDE traffic, secret and personal-data protection, prompt-injection controls, and tamper-evident decision logs that can stream to a SIEM. The company says deployments can be air-gapped and that a proof of concept typically takes about two weeks; its pricing page says deployments are scoped to the organisation rather than publishing a public price. These are vendor claims, not independently tested performance results.
- **Conflicts/uncertainty:** The 22 November launch date comes from a third-party launch directory, not a dated company announcement located in this scan. The official product page describes capabilities but does not expose an independent security assessment, deployment benchmark, or public price. The vendor has a direct commercial interest in positioning its product as an AI security control plane. Its comparisons with the wider category are vendor-authored.
- **Why this matters to my audience:** Teams deploying agents across coding tools, browser sessions and business applications need more than prompt-level guardrails. The operational question is who owns each agent identity, what data and tools it can reach, which policies are enforced at runtime, and where the evidence is retained. For African organisations with strict data-residency or connectivity requirements, the self-hosted/air-gapped claim is worth evaluating, but infrastructure, support and total cost must be tested locally.
- **Scores:** Relevance 5 / Actionability 4 / Novelty 4 / Total **13/15** (provisional; recency timestamp not verified).
- **Suggested slot:** **PLAYBOOK** — use as a timely product example to frame an agent-security control-plane evaluation checklist; do not treat launch-listing claims as independent proof.
- **Angle from my pool:** Threathunter360; AI Agent Security Readiness Audit; agent identity, permissions, tool inventory, prompt/context injection, logging and human approval.
- **Possible action for reader:** Inventory every AI agent, MCP server and coding assistant in one environment; map each identity to its sponsor, credentials, tools and data scope; test whether policy is enforced at the endpoint and whether decisions create reviewable audit evidence.

### [ITEM 2] — Tiquiz launches an AI quiz-to-email lead segmentation workflow

- **Source:** Tiquiz official website — https://tiquiz.io/ ; pricing — https://tiquiz.io/pricing ; launch-date listing: https://avalwhub.com/p/tiquiz
- **Author:** Product site identifies the company as Ethilife; no named author on the reviewed pages.
- **Published:** Official product pages have no visible publication timestamp. Avalwhub records the product as launched **22 November 2026**. Exact launch time and exact rolling-window qualification: **[UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Event date:** 22 November 2026 according to the launch directory; independently unconfirmed.
- **What happened:** Tiquiz is an AI quiz builder that generates questions and result profiles from a topic and audience brief, then captures leads and applies profile tags in connected email/CRM tools. Its official pages list direct connections to Systeme.io, GoHighLevel and Brevo, plus webhooks for other tools. The pricing page lists a free tier at $0 with one quiz, one survey, one Popquiz, 10 visible leads over a rolling 30-day period and one connection; paid tiers are listed at $17/month and $29/month, with annual prices displayed separately. The site says the free plan does not require a credit card; plan details and prices are vendor-published and may change.
- **Conflicts/uncertainty:** The 22 November launch date is from a third-party directory; the official pages do not establish the exact launch time or date of first availability. The site's conversion and comparative claims are vendor marketing; the 44.9% figure shown on the homepage is attributed to an Interact report and is not a Tiquiz-specific measured result. Pricing was verified on the product's pricing page during this scan, but regional taxes, payment availability and Naira billing were not established.
- **Why this matters to my audience:** This is a concrete example of a small-business marketing workflow where content, lead qualification and CRM tagging are joined without requiring a separate automation middle layer for supported integrations. For Nigerian SMEs and agencies, the relevant test is not whether AI can write a quiz; it is whether the lead data lands reliably in the existing CRM, whether consent and data handling are clear, and whether the subscription can be paid for and supported locally.
- **Scores:** Relevance 4 / Actionability 5 / Novelty 3 / Total **12/15** (provisional; recency timestamp not verified).
- **Suggested slot:** **QUICK HIT** — a focused example of AI-assisted lead capture and segmentation, subject to confirming the launch date and checking regional payment support.
- **Angle from my pool:** LOG_ON sales/marketing automation; CRM and payment/email/social integrations; workflow automation for SMEs.
- **Possible action for reader:** Take one existing lead magnet and map the path from visitor response to CRM record, tag, consent record and follow-up email; test a quiz-based alternative with a small audience and compare qualified leads, not just form completions.
